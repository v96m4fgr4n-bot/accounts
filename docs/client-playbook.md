# Client Operational Playbook

*A simple daily routine for cash-based small businesses*
Draft v3 — for owners of tuckshops, clothing stores, hardware shops and similar.

This is the plain-language, client-facing companion to `erp-blueprint.md`. When building the client-facing capture app, this is the source of truth for copy, tone, and what a client should actually see and do — the Blueprint is for the underlying engine.

## 1. Purpose
Written for a business that's often just the owner plus one or two staff, mostly trading in cash, with no formal system before now. Goal: a short daily routine that protects cash, gives a true picture of the business, and gets records ready for a loan application, a supplier, or ZIMRA registration.

## 2. Getting Set Up
No old records needed. Day one is a simple joint count: stock on hand, cash + bank balance, equipment owned, who owes the business money (including informal book credit) and who it owes. That becomes the starting point. Also establishes current formalization stage — registered, presumptive tax, or not yet registered — and council trading licence status.

## 3. Who Does What
| Who | Handles |
|---|---|
| Owner | Reviews the daily cash sheet, approves purchases/payments, signs off stock counts |
| Till/Sales Staff (if any) | Logs every sale as it happens, handles the float |
| Consulting team | Monthly review, tax filings, guidance as the business formalizes |

If it's just the owner, they play every role — the routine is built for that.

## 4. Daily Cash Routine — the core loop

**4.1 Start of Day** — count and record the opening float.

**4.2 During the Day** — every sale written down as it happens: what, how much, cash or "on account" to a known customer.

**4.3 End of Day**
1. Count all cash in the till.
2. Check against: opening float + cash sales for the day − cash paid out.
3. Record whether it matches, and by how much if not.
4. Bank what isn't needed for tomorrow's float, as soon as practical/safe.

If cash doesn't match: a small gap occasionally is normal; a pattern is worth attention. This daily check is the main protection when there's no second person to double-check the till.

## 5. Buying Stock
Everyday cash restocking: simple note — date, what, from whom, how much, receipt if available. For a credit/bulk supplier: proper supplier record + simple order log (even a WhatsApp confirmation works) to check deliveries against orders and invoices.

## 6. Selling to Customers
Every sale logged as it happens (4.2). Services (repairs, alterations, key cutting) logged the same way. Customer book credit written down properly — who, how much, when.

## 7. Managing Stock
Know the reorder point per key item, based on real sell-through. Count regularly (monthly is a good start). Solo counting: photograph the count sheet/shelves as a timestamped record in place of a second counter. Notice slow-moving stock early — it ties up cash.

## 8. Everyday Expenses
Logged the day they're paid: category, amount, date. Rent, electricity, transport, airtime, packaging, repairs, casual-help wages.

## 9. Paying Anyone Who Works With You
Every payment to staff/casual help written down: who, for what, how much. Foundation for formal payroll once employees are registered.

## 10. Banking
Opening a business bank account is a milestone worth planning for (usually required for VAT registration; helps prove trading history for a loan). Once banking, reconcile against the daily cash sheet each month. If holding both USD cash and ZWG, note the rate used whenever it differs from the last recorded rate.

## 11. Equipment Owned
Durable, valuable items (delivery vehicle, shelving, fridge, till device) recorded with cost and expected life. Small tools/consumables stay as ordinary expenses.

## 12. Staying Compliant
A simple calendar of everything due at the client's current stage — presumptive tax, VAT returns if registered, PAYE if employing staff formally, council licence renewal — flagged before each is due. This is the core of avoiding penalties: nothing missed because it wasn't tracked. Any penalty that does occur is logged separately so the cause gets fixed.

## 13. Monthly Check-In
- All sales, purchases, expenses for the month written up
- Cash and bank reconciled, gaps explained
- Who owes/is owed reviewed
- Stock count done if due
- Simple P&L reviewed together
- Compliance calendar checked

## 14. Approvals in a Small Team
With just the owner (plus 0–2 staff), the owner approves everything — correct at this size, not a gap. A second sign-off is introduced for larger amounts as staff grow.

| Decision | Right now |
|---|---|
| Everyday cash restocking | Owner or trusted staff, reviewed on daily cash sheet |
| Credit purchase/new supplier | Owner approves directly |
| Paying a supplier | Owner pays directly |
| Stock adjustment after a count | Owner reviews and approves |
| Paying staff/casual help | Owner approves directly |

## 15. Questions or Something Unusual
Anything that doesn't fit the normal routine — a big discrepancy, an unusual payment, a new kind of transaction — goes to the consulting team before it's recorded, rather than forced into a category that doesn't fit.
